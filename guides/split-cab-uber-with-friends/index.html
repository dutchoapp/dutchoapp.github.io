How to split a cab or Uber fare with friends

One person's card is on the app, everyone piled in, and three of you got dropped at different stops. Here's how to split the fare fairly without turning a ₹380 ride into a maths problem.

Guide · about 5 min read

Short version

If everyone rides roughly the same distance, split equally — it's fair and fast. If one person is dropped much further out (or much earlier), weight the split by distance instead. Whoever's account booked it pays the driver; everyone else settles their share over UPI. Log it once so the person who always books doesn't quietly become everyone's bank.

Cab splitting is the most common shared expense there is — and the one people most often "forget" to settle, because each ride feels too small to bother with. The problem is that they add up. The friend whose Uber account the group always uses can be ₹1,500 down by the end of the month without anyone noticing. The fix is a quick, consistent habit, not a per-ride negotiation.

Default to an equal split

For most rides — the group heading to the same restaurant, the airport run where everyone's on the same flight — an equal split is both fair and the least friction. Total fare divided by heads, done. Don't distance-weight a ride where everyone travelled together the whole way; you'll spend more time calculating than the difference is worth.

Equal splitting is also the right call for short hops. Chasing exact distances on a ₹120 auto ride is effort nobody wants. Save the precision for the rides where it actually matters.

Distance-weight the lopsided rides

Sometimes the ride isn't symmetric: you all get in together, but one person is dropped 2 km in and another rides 18 km to the far side of the city. Splitting that equally quietly overcharges the person who hopped out first.

For those, weight by roughly how far each person rode. You don't need GPS precision — approximate shares are fine and universally accepted:

  • Drop-order rule. The last person out rode the whole distance, so they carry the largest share; the first person out carries the smallest. Sketch it as "near / middle / far" shares rather than exact kilometres.
  • Leg-based split. For a multi-stop ride, split each leg only among the people still in the car. Everyone shares leg one; only the remaining riders share the final stretch. It's the most accurate and still easy to reason about.

The rule that saves the friendship

Whoever books never chases. If your account is the group default, the deal should be that the ride is logged the moment it ends, so settling up is automatic and you never have to send the awkward "hey, ₹95 for the cab?" text. The booking is a convenience you're offering — it shouldn't cost you money.

Booked it? Pay the driver, then settle up

The person whose app booked the ride pays the fare — that part's already handled by the app. What's left is everyone else settling their share to that person. With UPI this is seconds: share your UPI ID or a QR, and each rider pays their exact amount. The whole thing should be closed out before you've reached the door.

Stop the small rides adding up

The real trick with cabs isn't the maths of any single ride — it's not losing track across dozens of tiny ones. Ten forgotten ₹90 splits is ₹900 you never got back.

Log each ride the moment it ends and let a shared tally do the remembering.
